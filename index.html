Context and functional description

What was asked, and with which data.

My Content wants to encourage reading by recommending relevant content. The most critical feature of the MVP fits in one sentence: “as a user of the application, I receive a selection of five articles.” As there is no proprietary data yet, the MVP is built on the public News Portal User Interactions by Globo.com dataset.

Major constraint: the dataset is anonymised. An article has no title and no text, only an identifier and a 250-dimension vector. There are no ratings or reviews, only clicks, so the signal is implicit. Everything is done without reading the content, and the interface can only display identifiers, a category, a length and a date.

What the service does

For a given reader (user_id), it returns the n articles most likely to interest them (5 by default). It accepts a strategy (method), a region, a reading history supplied by the caller and a freshness filter.

  • Five strategies to choose from: popularity, content, collaborative ALS, SVD, hybrid — plus the mixed strategy, served by default.
  • New reader, or one with no usable history: popular articles, adapted to their region when it is known.
  • Priority to recent content: the recommendation window is recomputed periodically.
  • Never an empty list, whatever the request.
  • Stateless: for a reader the service does not know, the caller sends the history in the request.

The five approaches tested

How each one ranks the articles, with its advantages and drawbacks.

Popularity

most-read articles over a time window

The baseline to beat: it personalises nothing, so any more complex approach has to do better.

  • highest precision (HitRate@5 = 0.2525 over 1 hour)
  • no cold start
  • same list for every reader (personalisation 4%)
  • exposes 11 articles out of 364,047

Content-based filtering

profile = mean of the embeddings read, cosine against the catalogue
  • works from the first click, with no retraining
  • a new article can be recommended immediately
  • widest coverage: 0.27% of the catalogue
  • personalisation 96%
  • low precision: HitRate@5 = 0.0200
  • with no date restriction, suggests articles that are years old

Collaborative ALS

factorisation for implicit feedback (implicit library)
  • captures latent tastes that content similarity cannot see
  • element of surprise, outside the theme already read
  • personalisation 95%
  • HitRate@5 = 0.0415 on the test period
  • useless for a new reader or a new article
  • an article published two hours ago has almost no co-reads
  • sensitive, coupled settings

Collaborative SVD

prediction of ratings derived from clicks (Surprise library)

Same algorithm, three definitions of the rating: article stars (0.0015, does not rank), binary + 1 negative (0.0080), binary + 4 negatives (0.0815 on validation).

  • well posed, the best of the three variants
  • fast to train, few settings
  • does not reproduce: 0.0815 on validation, 0.0220 on test
  • same cold start as ALS

Mixed — retained for production

4 slots to recent popularity, 1 slot to content
  • the slot given to content costs no measurable precision
  • it multiplies catalogue coverage by 38
  • best precision/coverage compromise of the five approaches
  • limited personalisation: only 22%

Results

An evaluation protocol that does not let itself be flattered, and a result that changes the product's priority.

How we found out the first figures were wrong

First evaluation: ALS trained on 100% of the clicks, then measured by removing each reader's last click — a click the model had already seen. HitRate@5 = 0.2415. With a correct temporal split it falls to 0.0415: a factor of 5.8 of imaginary precision. The split is temporal, 60 / 20 / 20: settings are tuned on validation, and the test period is used only for the final measurement, once.

Four criteria, not one

Precision alone always picks popularity, which is twelve times more precise than content but exposes only 11 articles out of 364,047: a publisher loses its catalogue. Hence four criteria: precision (HitRate@5), catalogue coverage, personalisation (the difference between two readers' lists) and recall (Recall@5).

Limits, stated openly: cold start is not measured (only readers known at training time are evaluated), and all these figures are offline — the click-through rate on real recommendations remains the only true judge.

The retained recommendation system

What runs in production: the “mixed” strategy.

Five slots

  • Slots 1 to 4: the most-read articles of the last hour.
  • Slot 5: one article chosen by content, from a 6-hour pool.
  • Articles already read are always excluded.

Never an empty list

For a reader with no usable history, a four-level fallback cascade: region crossed with freshness, then freshness alone, then region alone, then the whole history.

A history passed in the request personalises even a reader the service has never seen.

Why this split

It costs about 1% of precision and multiplies the share of the catalogue exposed by 38: the best measured compromise between accuracy and coverage.

The application

A Streamlit interface that calls the Azure Function and displays the five articles.

  • Pick a reader from the list and get their five articles, with stars and reader counts.
  • Untick “recent articles”: the list changes — the measured freshness effect, live.
  • Switch strategy (content, ALS, SVD, hybrid): a different ranking.
  • Mark an article as read: the content profile is recomputed on demand, with no retraining.
  • Browse the catalogue of 364,047 articles: without it, the reader would only click what the model already suggests.
  • Register a new reader: recommended immediately, region optional.
  • Publish a new article: available within minutes. The PCA projection is saved with the catalogue (51 KB), so an article is added without refitting the PCA or recomputing the 364,047 existing vectors.

The application computes nothing: it calls the endpoint and displays the five identifiers — it is the Function that recommends.

  • Stateless. The Function keeps nothing between two requests: the profile of a reader it does not know arrives in the history parameter.
  • But not memoryless. The Recommender instance is reused from one invocation to the next: the 253 MB are read once per instance, at cold start.
  • Two accesses, not one. The binding re-reads its blob on every invocation, which is what 23 KB that change every hour needs. Applied to the 253 MB it would remove the cache: 8 s per call instead of 0.2 s, 40 times more.
  • Embeddings reduced by PCA: 250 dimensions brought down to 50, to fit within the limits of Azure's free tier.
  • numpy only at inference. scikit-learn, implicit and Surprise are used offline only, to produce the artifacts.
What separates a service from a demonstration. Four silent failures are each covered by a guard and a test: artifact drift (cache validity judged on the blob's size and date), a stale cache (the container is enumerated, not read from a fixed list), an inconsistent catalogue (any identifier outside the catalogue is filtered out) and interference between concurrent requests (the supplied history lives in a threading.local). None of them raises an error: the service would answer 200 with bad recommendations.

Industrialisation

What makes the MVP reproducible and verifiable.

  • Tests: 87 unit tests on synthetic artifacts, with no real data.
  • Continuous integration (GitHub Actions): tests, repository hygiene (no secrets, no artifact versioned by mistake) and Terraform validation, on every push and every pull request.
  • Deployed-service check: a script compares Azure's response to a reference record, because a service that answers is not a service that answers correctly.
  • Infrastructure described in Terraform: resource group, storage account and a Function on a Flex Consumption plan, with the connection string read from the resource rather than written in clear text.
  • A single recommendation core (src/recommender.py): the deployed copies are generated, and CI checks they are up to date.
  • Quality gate: publishing a retrained model is blocked if HitRate@5 drops by more than 10% against the versioned reference.
